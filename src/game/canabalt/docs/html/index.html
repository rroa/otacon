About Canabalt

Canabalt is the original one-button infinite runner: a lone figure sprints across the rooftops of a collapsing city, and the only control is jump. Hold longer to jump higher; clear the gaps; survive as far as you can. There is no winning — only your distance in metres, and your best.

This is a teaching-oriented C++ port. The source of truth is the official OSS release (ericjohnson/canabalt-ios, Objective-C on flixel-ios), kept under reference/ and studied closely so the port does not diverge from the original's feel. The game is written entirely against the Otacon engine via its IGame interface; everything game-specific — code, assets and these docs — lives under src/game/canabalt/.

Faithful. The simulation reproduces flixel's exact integrator, velocity model, swept-AABB collision and follow camera, plus Canabalt's exact player physics constants. With the default float scalar, a run is deterministic — the same seed yields the same city every time.

Building & running

Canabalt is the canabalt executable; it links the Otacon static library.

cmake -S . -B build          # first configure fetches the window library
cmake --build build -j8
./build/bin/canabalt

Windows: open the folder in Visual Studio (it configures from CMakeLists.txt) and run the canabalt.exe target, or use VS Code's CMake Tools. Vulkan on macOS: launch via tools/run.sh (sets the MoltenVK loader). Any backend plays identically.

How to play

You start on the title screen; press jump to begin. From then on you run automatically and ever faster — tap jump to hop a gap, hold it for a higher leap. Falling into a gap, smacking a wall, or being caught by a hazard ends the run.

  • Distance. The metres-run counter sits top-right.
  • Game over. A panel shows how far you ran and how you died. Press jump or R to run again, or click EXIT (top-right) to return to the title.
  • High score. Beat your best and a NEW RECORD badge appears; it's saved to assets/highscore.dat and persists between sessions.

The incremental modes

The build is staged as four runnable modes — the game assembled from a single box up to the full runner. Cycle with ]/[, or jump to one with 1–4.

  1. Static Box — a box on the ground. Geometry + camera only.
  2. Gravity — a box falls and lands (integrator + collision). Space to drop again; E bursts particles at the cursor.
  3. Run & Jump — the real Player auto-runs on flat ground; hold to jump; the camera follows. Full feel, no hazards.
  4. Infinite Run — the real game: the ported level generator, the parallax city, hazards, sprites, sound and the title/score flow.

The hold-to-jump window scales with speed: the faster you run, the higher you can leap. A hard landing makes the runner stumble and roll.

The level generator (Sequence.cpp)

A faithful port of Sequence.m. Two "sequences" leapfrog: each owns one wide building, and when one scrolls off the left it regenerates just past the other. Each building's gap, width and vertical drop derive from your current velocity.x and jumpLimit — so the level gets harder precisely as you get faster. The first building is a fixed launch hallway. Special archetypes:

  • Hallway — a tunnel; its windows smash (glass + sound) as you pass, doors line the back wall.
  • Collapse — the roof sinks the moment you step on it; ride it down and leap off before it drops away.
  • Bomb — whistles down and detonates on the roof; touching it is fatal.
  • Crane — a metal beam you cross like a platform (footsteps ring out as metal).
  • Billboard — a thin catwalk ledge under a damaged sign.
  • Leg — a giant mech leg stomps down and crushes the building below.

Rooftops also scatter obstacle crates and pigeons that flush as you approach; roof/floor caps and wall/window styles vary per building.

Background & foreground extras

Distant Easter eggs ride the skyline — a mothership, a dark tower, two marching mech walkers (venting smoke), and a jet that streaks past and rattles the screen — while a steel girder occasionally sweeps the foreground. A startup screen-quake shakes the camera as a run begins.

Presentation

Progressive sprite reveal (F6 / F8)

Peel the world between pure collision geometry and full sprites one layer at a time: geometry → player → buildings → background → full sprites. F8 jumps between all-geometry and all-sprites; C overlays the collision boxes at any level.

Sound & music

Sound effects (footsteps, jumps, window smashes, glass, the bomb/leg/collapse hazards, the jet, pigeon flaps, the death thud) are decoded in-house from the original .caf and mixed live. Three looping tracks are included — F9 mutes all audio, F10 cycles the track. The title screen has its own theme.

Assets

All game assets live under src/game/canabalt/assets/, loaded through the engine's in-house decoders — nothing is baked into the binary:

  • images/ — PNGs (player sheet, HUD glyphs, building/crane/billboard tiles, UI). images/raw/ holds the un-atlased source art.
  • sound/ — SFX as 22050 Hz mono 16-bit LPCM .caf; convert.sh regenerates them from the .mp3 via afconvert.
  • music/ — the looping tracks, converted the same way.
